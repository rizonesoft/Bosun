1. **P1, `scripts/lib/run_guard.py:1039`: Linux recovery can kill unrelated processes after PID reuse.** The ownership check relies on `process_started()`, which returns `0` for every live Linux process. A child that exits and whose PID is reused before a later watchdog tick therefore still passes as owned. The new termination loop also sends `SIGKILL` to descendants without rechecking their recorded identities. Reproduction: during a long WSL campaign, let a resumed child exit and its PID be reused before recovery. An in-memory probe confirmed that the reused identity passes and that the termination loop signals changed identities without checking them. Read real Linux start times and revalidate each termination target.

2. **P1, `scripts/lib/run_guard.py:265`: Slow termination loses operator cancellation and can launch a replacement afterward.** `stop_hung()` runs inside `watch_tick()`’s state lock. Termination can spend 60 seconds in `taskkill` plus 10 seconds checking survivors, while cancellation waits only five seconds for that lock. A `stop` prompt during this interval times out and fails open without recording the stop. Reproduction: make termination take six seconds, submit `UserPromptSubmit` with `stop` while it runs, then allow termination to succeed. An in-memory reproduction using the production lock deadline produced a cancellation timeout, `active=True`, and one replacement launch. Keep cancellation durable during termination and recheck it before spawning.
