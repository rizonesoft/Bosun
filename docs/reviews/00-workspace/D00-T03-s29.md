## Part 2. Excellence

The strongest alternative I would compare is a foreground PowerShell/.NET waiter combining `WaitHandle.WaitAny` for already-known completions and a timeout with `FileSystemWatcher` for mark changes. It can stay inside the operator’s session. The targets below are proposed benchmarks. I ran read-only, in-memory probes for this review, not a head-to-head benchmark or the file-writing test suite. [Microsoft WaitHandle.WaitAny](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/system.threading.waithandle.waitany) · [Microsoft FileSystemWatcher](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/system.io.filesystemwatcher)

1. **`suggestion` | Reduce completion latency.** A gate that exits just after a poll waits nearly a second for the next observation. Target a 95th-percentile exit-to-report time below 250 ms across 100 local gate exits, including exits with another gate still live.
2. **`suggestion` | Reduce idle reads.** A quiet nine-minute wait schedules roughly 540 rounds of state, mark, and process checks. Target at least 90% fewer reads over the same interval while retaining a recovery scan for missed notifications.
